## Why Do Teeth Get Stained?

Before understanding how whitening works, it helps to understand why teeth discolour in the first place.

**Surface stains (extrinsic)** build up on the outer enamel from:
- **Tea and coffee** — tannins bind to enamel and are one of the most common causes of staining in India
- **Pan and betel nut** — creates deep, persistent reddish-brown stains, particularly common in Odisha
- **Tobacco** (smoking or chewing) — heavy yellow-brown deposits that penetrate deeply over time
- **Certain foods** — turmeric, dark curries, berries

**Deep stains (intrinsic)** occur within the tooth structure itself:
- Ageing — the outer enamel naturally thins over time, letting the yellower dentine beneath show through
- Tetracycline antibiotics taken during childhood — can cause grey or brown banding within the tooth
- Fluorosis (too much fluoride during tooth development) — white spots or brown patches
- Trauma to a tooth — can cause internal darkening

**The key distinction:** professional teeth whitening is highly effective on extrinsic stains and on age-related intrinsic yellowing. It is less effective on tetracycline staining and fluorosis, where veneers may give better results. ## Professional Teeth Whitening vs Home Kits: What's the Difference?

Walk into any pharmacy or supermarket and you'll find whitening toothpastes, strips, and "whitening kits" making bold promises. Here's an honest comparison:

| | Professional (ZDentistry) | Over-the-Counter |
|---|---|---|
| Active agent | 25–35% hydrogen peroxide | 3–10% hydrogen peroxide |
| Shade improvement | Up to 6–10 shades | 1–2 shades at best |
| Speed | 1 hour (in-clinic) or 2 weeks (take-home) | 4–8 weeks |
| Custom fit | Yes — impressions taken | No — one-size trays |
| Pre-treatment check | Yes — decay/gum issues identified | No |
| Sensitivity management | Desensitising agents used | None |
| Results | Dramatic, even, consistent | Variable, often patchy |

The critical difference is **concentration**. OTC products are legally limited to very low concentrations of peroxide, which is why results are minimal. Professional whitening gels used under dental supervision are significantly stronger — and because the procedure is overseen by a dentist who has first confirmed your teeth and gums are healthy, the process is both safer and far more effective.

Whitening toothpastes work differently again — they rely on mild abrasives rather than bleaching agents, and they are better at maintaining results than achieving them.

## How Long Do Whitening Results Last?

Professional teeth whitening results typically last **1–2 years** before a noticeable re-darkening occurs — longer if you avoid heavy staining foods and drinks and don't smoke.

**To extend your results:**
- Rinse with water after tea, coffee, or staining foods
- Use a whitening toothpaste for maintenance (not as a primary whitening treatment)
- Book a professional clean every 6 months to remove surface build-up
- Do a brief touch-up with take-home trays every 6–12 months

## Who Is a Good Candidate for Teeth Whitening?

Whitening works best if:
- Your teeth are generally healthy — no active decay or gum disease (these must be treated first)
- Your staining is extrinsic (surface) or age-related yellowing
- You have natural teeth (whitening does not change the colour of crowns, veneers, or fillings)
- You are not pregnant or breastfeeding

Teeth whitening is **not recommended** for children under 16.

If you have crowns or fillings on visible front teeth, whitening the natural teeth around them may create a mismatch. In these cases, Dr. Zuben will discuss whether whitening still makes sense or whether a more comprehensive cosmetic plan is better.

## Does Teeth Whitening Damage Enamel?

When performed correctly under dental supervision, professional whitening does **not damage enamel**. The hydrogen peroxide penetrates the enamel to oxidise the stain molecules inside — it does not remove or thin the enamel surface.

The most common side effect is **temporary sensitivity** — a mild zing with cold water or air for 1–2 days after treatment. This resolves on its own. Patients who already have sensitive teeth may experience slightly more sensitivity; we use desensitising agents in these cases.

Overuse of whitening — particularly unsupervised use of high-concentration gels — can cause enamel erosion and gum irritation. This is another reason professional supervision matters.
