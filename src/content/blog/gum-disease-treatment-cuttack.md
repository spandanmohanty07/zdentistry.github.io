## What Is Gum Disease?

Gum disease is a bacterial infection of the tissues surrounding and supporting your teeth. It develops in two main stages:

### Stage 1: Gingivitis (Reversible)
Gingivitis is inflammation of the gums caused by plaque buildup at the gumline. At this stage:
- Gums look red, swollen, or puffy
- Gums bleed when you brush or floss
- There may be mild bad breath
- **The bone and connective tissue are not yet affected**

Gingivitis is **100% reversible** with professional cleaning and improved home care.

### Stage 2: Periodontitis (Requires Treatment)
If gingivitis is left untreated, it progresses to periodontitis. Bacteria penetrate below the gumline, forming pockets between teeth and gums. These pockets harbour bacteria that destroy bone and connective tissue. Signs include:
- Gum recession (teeth look longer)
- Deep pockets between teeth and gums
- Loose or shifting teeth
- Persistent bad breath (halitosis)
- Pus between teeth and gums
- Tooth loss in advanced cases

Periodontitis **cannot be reversed** — but it can be controlled and stabilised with the right treatment.

## What Causes Gum Disease?

The primary cause is **plaque** — the sticky bacterial film that forms on teeth. Other contributing factors include:

- **Poor oral hygiene** — irregular brushing and no flossing
- **Tobacco use** — smoking significantly increases gum disease risk and slows healing
- **Diabetes** — uncontrolled blood sugar worsens gum disease
- **Hormonal changes** — pregnancy, menopause, and puberty make gums more sensitive
- **Certain medications** — some drugs cause gum overgrowth or dry mouth
- **Genetics** — some people are more susceptible despite good hygiene
- **Stress** — weakens the immune system's response to infection
- **Crooked or crowded teeth** — harder to clean properly

## Symptoms of Gum Disease to Watch For

See a dentist promptly if you experience any of these:

- Gums that bleed when brushing or eating hard foods
- Red, swollen, or tender gums
- Persistent bad breath that doesn't improve with brushing
- Gums pulling away from teeth
- Sensitivity at the gum line
- Loose teeth or changes in your bite
- Gaps forming between teeth

## Gum Disease and Your Overall Health

Research has established strong links between gum disease and systemic health conditions:

- **Heart disease**: Oral bacteria can enter the bloodstream and contribute to arterial inflammation
- **Diabetes**: Gum disease makes blood sugar harder to control — a two-way relationship
- **Pregnancy complications**: Periodontitis is associated with premature birth and low birth weight
- **Respiratory disease**: Inhaling oral bacteria can worsen lung infections
- **Alzheimer's disease**: Emerging research links periodontal bacteria to cognitive decline

Your mouth is the gateway to your body. Healthy gums support healthy overall health.

## What to Expect After Gum Treatment

After deep cleaning, it is normal to experience:
- Mild soreness or sensitivity for 1–3 days
- Slight gum bleeding for 24 hours
- Sensitivity to hot and cold as gums heal

**Tips for recovery:**
- Rinse gently with warm salt water twice daily for a few days
- Use a soft-bristled toothbrush
- Avoid hard, crunchy foods for 1–2 days
- Take prescribed antibiotics as directed
- Do not smoke — it significantly impairs healing

## How to Prevent Gum Disease

Prevention is always simpler than treatment:

1. **Brush twice daily** with fluoride toothpaste — for at least 2 minutes each time
2. **Floss daily** — gets between teeth where brushes can't reach
3. **Use an antibacterial mouthwash** if recommended by your dentist
4. **Get a professional clean every 6 months** at ZDentistry
5. **Quit smoking** — the single biggest lifestyle change you can make for gum health
6. **Manage diabetes and other health conditions** that affect gum health
7. **Eat a balanced diet** rich in vitamins C and D
