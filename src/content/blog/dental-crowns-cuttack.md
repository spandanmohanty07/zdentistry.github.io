## What Is a Dental Crown?

A dental crown — commonly called a **tooth cap** — is a custom-made covering that fits over a damaged, decayed, or weakened tooth, restoring it to its original shape, size, strength, and appearance.

Think of it like a protective helmet for your tooth. It encases the entire visible portion of the tooth above the gum line, protecting it from further damage while restoring full function.

## When Do You Need a Dental Crown?

Dental crowns are recommended in a variety of situations:

### 1. After a Root Canal
A tooth that has undergone root canal treatment becomes more brittle and prone to fracture. A crown placed over it restores full strength and protects it from cracking — extending the life of the treated tooth significantly.

### 2. Large or Broken Fillings
When a filling is too large or a tooth is broken, a crown provides much stronger protection than re-filling the tooth.

### 3. Severely Decayed Tooth
When decay is too extensive for a filling but the tooth root is still healthy and worth saving, a crown can restore the tooth to full function.

### 4. Cracked or Fractured Tooth
A cracked tooth that causes pain on biting — but hasn't yet required a root canal — is often best protected with a crown, which holds the tooth together and prevents the crack from worsening.

### 5. Worn-Down Teeth
Teeth worn down by bruxism (teeth grinding), acid erosion, or heavy use can be built back to their correct shape and height with crowns.

### 6. Cosmetic Improvement
Crowns can be placed for aesthetic reasons — to cover severely discoloured, misshapen, or poorly formed teeth where other cosmetic options like veneers are not suitable.

### 7. To Support a Dental Bridge
When replacing a missing tooth with a traditional dental bridge, the teeth on either side of the gap are crowned to anchor the bridge.

### 8. On Top of a Dental Implant
After a dental implant is placed, a crown is attached to the implant post to form the visible, functional tooth.

## Types of Dental Crowns Available at ZDentistry, Cuttack

Choosing the right crown material depends on the tooth's location, your bite, aesthetic preferences, and budget. Dr. Zuben will guide you through the best option for your specific situation.

### Zirconia Crowns (Most Popular)
- **Material**: Zirconium dioxide — an extremely strong ceramic
- **Appearance**: Natural-looking white — excellent for front and back teeth
- **Strength**: Exceptional — withstands heavy biting forces
- **Best for**: Most patients, especially those who want strength and aesthetics
- **Longevity**: 15+ years with proper care

### Ceramic / Porcelain Crowns
- **Material**: All-ceramic porcelain
- **Appearance**: The most natural-looking option — mimics enamel translucency
- **Strength**: Good for front teeth; less suitable for heavy-load back teeth
- **Best for**: Front teeth where cosmetics are the priority

### Porcelain-Fused-to-Metal (PFM) Crowns
- **Material**: Metal substructure with porcelain outer coating
- **Appearance**: Natural-looking but may show a dark line at the gum over time as gums recede
- **Strength**: Very strong
- **Best for**: Back teeth requiring durability, or patients where cost is a consideration

### Metal Crowns (Gold or Base Metal Alloy)
- **Material**: Gold alloy or base metal alloy
- **Appearance**: Metallic — highly visible
- **Strength**: Extremely durable — the longest-lasting option
- **Best for**: Back teeth (molars) where appearance is less important, or patients with heavy bite forces

## The Dental Crown Procedure at ZDentistry

Getting a dental crown typically takes **two appointments** at our CDA Sector-9 clinic:

### Appointment 1: Preparation & Impression

**Step 1: Examination**
We assess the tooth, take X-rays, and confirm a crown is the right treatment. Any existing decay or infection is treated first.

**Step 2: Tooth Preparation**
The tooth is shaped under local anaesthesia — a thin, even layer is removed from all sides to create space for the crown to fit over it. If the tooth is severely damaged, a core build-up material may be placed first to create a stable foundation.

**Step 3: Impression**
A precise impression (mould) of your prepared tooth is taken. This is sent to our dental laboratory where your custom crown is crafted to exact specifications — matching your bite and the shape of your surrounding teeth.

**Step 4: Temporary Crown**
A temporary crown is placed on the prepared tooth to protect it while your permanent crown is being made (typically 1–2 weeks).

### Appointment 2: Crown Placement

**Step 5: Fitting**
The permanent crown is checked for fit, bite, and appearance. Adjustments are made as needed.

**Step 6: Cementing**
Once you and Dr. Zuben are satisfied with the fit and look, the crown is permanently cemented in place.

The entire process is straightforward and, with local anaesthesia, completely comfortable.

## How Long Does a Dental Crown Last?

With proper care, a well-placed dental crown can last:

- **Zirconia crowns**: 15–25 years
- **Ceramic crowns**: 10–15 years
- **PFM crowns**: 10–15 years
- **Metal crowns**: 20+ years

Longevity depends on oral hygiene, bite forces, and avoiding habits that stress the crown (like teeth grinding or biting hard objects). Regular check-ups at ZDentistry allow us to monitor crown condition and address any issues early.

## Caring for Your Dental Crown

A crown requires the same care as your natural teeth — and a little extra attention at the margins (where the crown meets the gum):

1. **Brush twice daily** with a soft-bristled toothbrush
2. **Floss daily** — especially at the gum line around the crown
3. **Avoid extremely hard foods** — ice, hard sweets, bones
4. **Wear a night guard** if you grind your teeth — grinding is the fastest way to damage a crown
5. **Visit ZDentistry for regular check-ups** — we monitor the crown and the gum tissue around it

## Dental Crown vs Tooth Filling — Which Is Right for You?

| | Filling | Crown |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Small to medium decay | Large decay, broken or root-treated teeth |
| Coverage | Partial | Full tooth coverage |
| Strength | Moderate | Very high |
| Longevity | 5–10 years | 10–25 years |
| Cost | Lower | Higher — but better long-term value |

A filling is appropriate for smaller repairs. When the tooth is significantly compromised, a crown is the stronger, longer-lasting solution. Choosing a filling when a crown is needed often results in the tooth breaking later — at greater cost and complexity.
