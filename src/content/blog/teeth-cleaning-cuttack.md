## Why Professional Teeth Cleaning Matters More Than You Think

Brushing and flossing at home is essential — but it is not enough on its own. Over time, plaque hardens into **tartar (calculus)**, which cannot be removed by a toothbrush. Tartar buildup leads to gum disease, bad breath, and eventually tooth loss.

Professional teeth cleaning at ZDentistry in Cuttack removes tartar, polishes your teeth, and gives our team the chance to spot problems before they become serious.

---

## What Is Professional Teeth Cleaning?

Professional teeth cleaning — also called **dental scaling and polishing** — is a procedure where our dental team removes:

- **Plaque** — the sticky bacterial film that forms on teeth
- **Tartar (calculus)** — hardened plaque that builds up at and below the gumline
- **Stains** — from tea, coffee, tobacco, and food

The result: cleaner teeth, healthier gums, and fresher breath.

---

## Teeth Cleaning vs Teeth Whitening: What's the Difference?

Many patients in Cuttack confuse these two procedures.

| | Teeth Cleaning | Teeth Whitening |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Remove tartar, plaque, stains | Lighten tooth colour |
| Result | Healthier gums, natural colour | Visibly whiter teeth |
| Frequency | Every 6 months | As needed |
| Health benefit | Yes — prevents gum disease | Cosmetic only |
| Recommended for | Everyone | Those seeking whiter teeth |

Teeth cleaning is a **health procedure** first and foremost. Whitening is cosmetic. Many patients do both for maximum results.

## What Happens During a Teeth Cleaning at ZDentistry?

Here is exactly what to expect during your appointment at our CDA Sector-9 clinic:

### Step 1: Examination
Dr. Zuben Mohanty or our dental hygienist examines your teeth and gums before cleaning. X-rays may be taken if needed to check for hidden issues.

### Step 2: Scaling
Using an ultrasonic scaler and/or hand instruments, we carefully remove tartar from all tooth surfaces — including below the gumline. You may feel light vibration and water spray. It is not painful, though some patients with sensitive teeth or heavy tartar feel mild sensitivity.

### Step 3: Polishing
A rotating brush with gritty toothpaste (prophylaxis paste) polishes away surface stains and smooths tooth enamel, making it harder for plaque to adhere.

### Step 4: Flossing
Professional flossing clears debris from between teeth that instruments might have loosened.

### Step 5: Rinse and Review
We rinse your mouth and share observations about your gum health, areas to focus on at home, and when to return.

**Total appointment time: 30–60 minutes.**

## Does Teeth Cleaning Hurt?

For most patients, professional cleaning is comfortable. You may feel:
- Pressure from the scaler
- Light sensitivity if your gums are already inflamed
- Mild cold sensitivity from the water spray

Patients with **heavy tartar buildup or gum disease** may experience more sensitivity during their first deep cleaning. This improves significantly with regular 6-monthly cleans.

If you are nervous about the procedure, let us know — our team at ZDentistry is experienced in treating anxious patients gently.

## How Often Should You Get Your Teeth Cleaned?

The standard recommendation is **every 6 months** for most adults. However, some patients may need more frequent cleaning:

- **Every 3–4 months**: If you have active gum disease or are prone to heavy tartar buildup
- **Every 6 months**: For most healthy adults
- **Annually**: Only for patients with excellent home hygiene and minimal tartar (rare)

For children, we recommend cleanings from age 3–4, or whenever their first teeth are fully grown in.

## Signs You Need a Cleaning Soon

Don't wait for your next scheduled appointment if you notice:

- **Yellow or brown stains** on teeth
- **Bad breath** that persists despite brushing
- **Bleeding gums** when you brush or floss
- **Swollen, red, or tender gums**
- **Visible tartar** (rough, hard deposits near the gum line)
- **Loose teeth** or gum recession

These are signs of gum disease that require prompt professional attention.

## Deep Cleaning (Root Planing) — When Regular Cleaning Isn't Enough

For patients with moderate to severe gum disease, a standard clean may not be sufficient. **Deep cleaning (scaling and root planing)** involves cleaning the root surfaces below the gumline to remove bacteria and allow the gums to heal.
